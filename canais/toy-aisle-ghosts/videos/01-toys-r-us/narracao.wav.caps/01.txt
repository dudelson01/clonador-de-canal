The story starts in nineteen forty eight, in Washington, D.C. A twenty five year old war veteran named Charles Lazarus opened a small shop below his father's bicycle store. It wasn't a toy store. It sold baby furniture. Cribs. Strollers. He called it Children's Bargain Town.
It was perfect timing. Soldiers were coming home, and America was having babies like never before.
But Lazarus noticed a problem. A family buys a crib once. Maybe twice. Then they never come back. Toys were different. Toys broke. Kids grew out of them. And every birthday, every Christmas, the parents came back for more.
So he did something nobody else was doing. He looked at the new supermarkets popping up across America, with their giant aisles and shopping carts, and asked a simple question. Why not a supermarket, but for toys?
In nineteen fifty seven, he opened his first store that sold only toys. He gave it a name that sounded like a kid wrote it, with the R turned backwards. Toys"R"Us.
