In the nineties, Walmart and Target figured out something clever. They didn't need to make money on toys. They could sell the hottest toys at cost, or even at a loss, just to get parents through the door. Once you were inside, you'd buy groceries, clothes and everything else.
Toys"R"Us couldn't do that. Toys were all it sold. In nineteen ninety eight, for the first time in more than a decade, it lost its crown. Walmart sold seventeen point four percent of America's toys. Toys"R"Us sold sixteen point eight.
It was still a giant. But now it was a giant with a problem. And its solution to the next problem would turn out even worse.
