In September of two thousand seventeen, Toys"R"Us filed for bankruptcy. The plan was to restructure, survive Christmas, and come back stronger.
But Christmas went badly. Sales fell short. Suppliers got nervous. The lenders lost patience.
And on March fifteenth, twenty eighteen, the company announced it would liquidate. Every store in America would close.
Exactly one week later, on March twenty second, Charles Lazarus died at the age of ninety four. The man who had turned a crib shop into the biggest toy store in the world lived just long enough to see it end.
The liquidation sales lasted a few months. Shelves emptied. Signs came down. By the end of June, the last stores had locked their doors.
Around thirty thousand workers lost their jobs. And because of how bankruptcy works, they didn't get severance. Later that year, after public pressure, two of the owners set up a twenty million dollar hardship fund for former employees. For many workers, it was far less than what they had lost.
