Now we get to the real answer. In March of two thousand five, three investment firms, Bain Capital, KKR and the real estate company Vornado, agreed to buy Toys"R"Us for six point six billion dollars.
But here's the catch. Most of that money wasn't theirs. It was borrowed. It's called a leveraged buyout. And in a leveraged buyout, the debt doesn't stay with the buyers. It gets loaded onto the company they just bought.
Overnight, Toys"R"Us went from a struggling company to a struggling company carrying around five billion dollars of debt.
Just the interest on that debt cost about four hundred million dollars a year. Every single year. Before paying a single employee. Before fixing a single store.
That's money that could have gone into better stores, a better website, better prices. Instead, it went to the lenders. The stores started to look tired. The shelves got messier. The website stayed clunky. And customers noticed.
In December of two thousand fifteen, even the Times Square flagship closed its doors. The rent there was reported at around forty two million dollars a year. The company simply couldn't afford its own crown jewel anymore.
Then came the moment everyone knew was coming.
