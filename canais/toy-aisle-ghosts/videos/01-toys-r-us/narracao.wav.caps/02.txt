The formula was simple. Huge stores. Every toy you could imagine, stacked to the ceiling. Low prices. And a giraffe.
Geoffrey the giraffe had started out in the ads for that old baby furniture shop. Now he became the face of an empire.
Through the seventies and eighties, Toys"R"Us spread across the country. It became what business people call a category killer. Small toy shops simply couldn't compete. For roughly fifteen years, it was the biggest toy seller in America.
And it had a jingle every kid in America could sing. I don't wanna grow up, I'm a Toys"R"Us kid.
If you were a kid in the eighties or nineties, you remember the feeling. The video game aisle, where you grabbed a paper ticket and took it to the register. The bikes hanging from the ceiling. The wish list you circled in the catalog. It wasn't just a store. It was the place where Christmas started.
In two thousand one, the company opened its crown jewel. A four story flagship in Times Square, with a sixty foot Ferris wheel inside and a giant animatronic T-rex.
But by then, the empire was already cracking. And the first crack came from a place nobody in the toy business was watching.
