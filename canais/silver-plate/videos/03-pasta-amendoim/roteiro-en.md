## Hook
[VISUAL: a spoon slowly lifting creamy peanut butter out of a jar.]
Two spoonfuls of peanut butter a day. That's all it takes to change at least four things in your body after sixty. Three of them are good news. One of them isn't, and almost nobody talks about it.

[VISUAL: an older man spreading peanut butter on toast in a sunny kitchen.]
Today, we'll go through all four, with real studies, not miracle promises. You'll also learn how much to eat, how to choose a good jar in ten seconds, and the truth about that popular claim that peanut butter before bed melts belly fat.

[VISUAL: title card.]
Quick note: this video is general education, not medical advice. All sources are in the description. If you have kidney disease, a peanut allergy or take medication, talk to your doctor first.

## What's inside two spoonfuls
[CADERNO: a jar of peanut butter with labels around it.]
Let's start with what's actually in a serving. Two tablespoons of smooth peanut butter, about thirty-two grams, have roughly one hundred ninety calories, about seven grams of protein, sixteen grams of fat and around two grams of fiber {F1}.

[3D: peanut particles breaking down into glowing nutrients in the stomach.]
Most of that fat is the unsaturated kind, similar to what you find in nuts and olive oil. And peanuts also bring potassium, magnesium and vitamin E. In other words, it's a small spoon with a lot packed inside, and that's exactly why it can help you, or work against you.

## Change one: your muscles get building blocks
[3D: thigh muscle receiving glowing protein particles.]
The first change: your muscles get more building blocks. Seven grams of protein may not sound like much. But after sixty, the body needs more protein than before, not less: at least one point zero to one point two grams per kilo every day {F2}.

[CADERNO: toast plus peanut butter plus a glass of milk, "≈ 20 g".]
On its own, peanut butter won't get you to the twenty-five to thirty grams per meal that muscles respond to best {F3}. But it's a great team player. Put two spoonfuls on whole-grain toast and drink a glass of milk, and you're already close to twenty grams of protein at breakfast.

[VISUAL: older woman climbing stairs at home with confidence.]
And remember, muscles need movement too. Protein gives them the materials. Walking, climbing stairs and standing up from a chair tell them to use those materials.

## Change two: you stay full longer
[VISUAL: breakfast table with peanut butter toast and coffee, morning light.]
The second change surprised researchers. In a study at Purdue University, women at high risk of type two diabetes ate the same breakfast on different days, sometimes with about three tablespoons of peanut butter, sometimes without {F4}.

[CADERNO: two curves after lunch, one lower, with a small peanut drawing.]
When they had peanut butter at breakfast, they reported less desire to eat in the hours that followed. And their blood sugar rose less after lunch, a meal they ate hours later {F4}.

[CADERNO: "15 women · small study".]
To be honest, it was a small study, with only fifteen women. But it fits what many people notice: a breakfast with protein and fat keeps you satisfied much longer than toast and jam alone. And when you're less hungry, it's easier not to snack on cookies before lunch.

## Change three: possible long-term benefits
[VISUAL: older couple walking in a park at sunset.]
The third change is about the long term. In the Nurses' Health Study, researchers followed almost eighty-four thousand women for sixteen years. Those who ate peanut butter five or more times a week had about a twenty-one percent lower risk of developing type two diabetes than those who almost never ate it {F5}.

[CADERNO: "83,818 women · 16 years · −21% diabetes risk".]
Another large study followed people in the United States and in Shanghai, China. In the American group, those who ate the most peanuts had a twenty-one percent lower risk of dying during the study period, especially from heart disease {F6}.

[CADERNO: a magnifying glass over a chart, "link, not proof".]
Now, these are observational studies. They show a link, not proof. People who eat peanuts may have other healthy habits too. But the results point in the same direction across very different groups of people, and that's worth paying attention to.

## Does it raise cholesterol?
[VISUAL: a jar of peanut butter next to a stick of butter on a kitchen counter.]
A question many people over sixty ask: is peanut butter bad for cholesterol? Here's something that surprises a lot of people. Peanut butter has no cholesterol at all, because it comes from a plant. Cholesterol in food only comes from animal products.

[CADERNO: a heart with two arrows, "butter" and "peanut butter".]
What matters more is the type of fat. Peanut butter is mostly unsaturated fat. Health organizations recommend replacing saturated fats, like butter, with unsaturated ones, to help keep cholesterol in a healthier range. So spreading peanut butter on your toast instead of butter is generally a smart swap. Just keep the portion small, because the calories are similar.

## Change four: the one that isn't good
[VISUAL: a large spoon overflowing with peanut butter next to an open jar.]
And now the fourth change, the one almost nobody mentions: if you're not careful, peanut butter can make you gain weight. It's one of the most calorie-dense foods in your kitchen.

[CADERNO: two spoons "190 kcal" and four spoons "≈ 380 kcal".]
Two spoonfuls are about one hundred ninety calories. But a lot of people don't measure. They dip the spoon straight into the jar a few times, and four heaping spoons can easily pass three hundred eighty calories, almost like a small meal.

[VISUAL: a jar of peanut butter replacing a plate of cookies on a table.]
So does peanut butter make you fat? Only if you add it on top of everything you already eat. The researchers from the diabetes study made exactly this recommendation: use nuts to replace less healthy foods, like refined bread or processed meats, not in addition to them {F5}.

## How to choose a good jar
[VISUAL: two jars of peanut butter side by side on a supermarket shelf, labels blurred.]
Here's the ten-second test at the supermarket: turn the jar around and read the ingredients. The best list is short: peanuts, and maybe salt.

[CADERNO: an ingredient list with "sugar" and "hydrogenated oil" crossed out.]
Many popular brands add sugar and hydrogenated or palm oils to make it sweeter and creamier. You don't need that. And if you watch your blood pressure, look for a version with no added salt.

[VISUAL: a jar of natural peanut butter with a layer of oil on top being stirred.]
One more tip: natural peanut butter often has a layer of oil on top. That's normal. Just stir it well, and keep the jar in the refrigerator after opening if you want it to separate less.

## Make it at home in five minutes
[VISUAL: roasted peanuts being poured into a food processor.]
If you can't find a jar with just peanuts, or you want to save money, you can make peanut butter at home. All you need is roasted peanuts without skins and a food processor or a strong blender.

[VISUAL: peanut butter turning creamy inside a food processor.]
Blend for about five to ten minutes. First it turns into crumbs, then a thick paste, and then, suddenly, it becomes creamy. Stop every couple of minutes to scrape the sides. Add a pinch of salt if you like, and that's it.

[VISUAL: a glass jar of homemade peanut butter with a spoon, on a wooden table.]
Store it in a clean glass jar in the refrigerator, and it keeps for a few weeks. No sugar, no added oils, and you know exactly what's inside.

## How much and how to eat it
[CADERNO: a tablespoon with "1–2 per day".]
For most people, one to two tablespoons a day is a sensible amount. Use a real measuring spoon for a week, just to see what a portion looks like. If you're trying to lose weight, one spoon is enough. But if you're losing weight without wanting to, which is common in older age, two spoons a day is one of the easiest ways to add good calories and protein. In that case, talk to your doctor too.

[VISUAL: whole-grain toast with peanut butter and banana slices.]
Some easy ways to eat it: on whole-grain toast with banana slices. Stirred into warm oatmeal. As a dip for apple slices. Or blended into a smoothie with milk and a banana, which is great if chewing is difficult.

[VISUAL: a bowl of vegetables with a creamy peanut sauce.]
You can even mix a spoon with a little warm water, lemon and garlic to make a quick sauce for vegetables or chicken.

## What about before bed?
[VISUAL: a dim bedroom with a small plate on the nightstand at night.]
Now, about those videos promising that one spoon of peanut butter before bed melts belly fat like a weight-loss drug. I looked for the evidence, and no food melts belly fat on its own, peanut butter included.

[VISUAL: a small slice of toast with peanut butter and a glass of milk on a nightstand.]
If you get hungry at night, a small portion of peanut butter on toast is a much better choice than sweets, because the protein and fat keep you satisfied. Just count it as part of your day, not as a magic trick.

## Who should be careful
[3D: kidneys with a soft warning glow.]
Now, the honest part. If you have kidney disease, peanuts contain potassium and phosphorus, two minerals you may need to limit. Ask your doctor or dietitian how much is safe for you.

[VISUAL: an older person drinking water while eating toast.]
If you have trouble swallowing, thick peanut butter can stick in the throat. Spread it thin, mix it into oatmeal or smoothies, and always have something to drink with it.

[VISUAL: a jar of peanuts with a red warning sign.]
And of course, if you're allergic to peanuts, stay away completely. A peanut allergy can be serious at any age.

## Summary
[CADERNO: four small drawings: a muscle, a clock, a heart, a scale.]
So, what really happens when you eat peanut butter every day after sixty? One: your muscles get extra protein. Two: you stay full longer, and your blood sugar may rise less after the next meal. Three: large studies link it to lower diabetes risk and longer life. And four: if you don't measure, it can make you gain weight.

[VISUAL: older woman enjoying peanut butter toast at a sunny window.]
One to two measured spoonfuls, from a jar with just peanuts, replacing something less healthy. That's the smart way to do it.
